The same gender question reads differently at each rung

A probe reads gender from the activation a, zeroing a along the probe's directions moves the model's predictions, and rerunning one bio with a changed computes that bio's counterfactual about a. Each tab shows the sentence its evidence supports and what it takes to move that sentence up one rung.

Setup. 200 synthetic biographies, half about a woman (she) and half about a man (he). The model reads each bio and predicts the person's profession, nurse or surgeon. At one layer ℓ we record the activation a. Besides gender, each bio carries a cue, care words (“comfort”, “families”) versus hospital words (“theatre”, “schedule”), that travels with gender in these bios, and profession content that does not. All numbers are illustrative toy values.

“Does setting these activations change the profession the model predicts?”

INLP (iterative nullspace projection): train a probe, delete its direction from a, train a fresh probe on what is left, and delete that direction too. This toy stops once a fresh probe scores below 75%. Then we rerun every bio with the edited a and compare the model's p(nurse) with the unedited run.

To move this claim up a rung, you need: about a, nothing more, since one bio's rerun already is the counterfactual about a (rung-3 tab, ①); about gender, either a removed direction that is gender alone and whose zero is the he value, or a twin bio that changes gender and nothing else. The toy meets the zero condition only because it codes he as 0; projection in a real model sets the direction to zero, which for centred activations is the midpoint between she and he, a value no bio had.

To move the gender claim onto this rung, you need: a removed direction that is gender alone and whose zero is the he value (then ① equals ②; true here only because the toy codes he as 0), or a twin bio that changes gender and nothing else (then ③ equals ②). None of these conditions shows up in the rerun itself.

Toy model, seeded and deterministic. Each bio has gender g (she = 1, he = 0), cue c (care words high, hospital words low; correlation ρ with g) and profession content p. The activation is a = R·(g, c, p) + small noise, with R a fixed rotation, so no neuron lines up with a concept. The readout is the nurse logit y = bg·ag + bc·ac + bp·ap − 1, where ag, ac, ap are a's coordinates along the three concepts. The probe is linear discriminant analysis. Built for this tutorial to illustrate the paper's “Decodability ≠ Model Use” gap on the tutorial's running example; it is not an experiment from the paper, and the bios are not Bias-in-Bios data.

What this shows

The three tabs ask about one population of bios and one activation. On the rung-1 tab the probe reads gender almost perfectly in both hidden worlds, at the same accuracy, and it must be the same: the two worlds share every activation and differ only in what the model's readout does with them, so no statistic of a can tell them apart. On the rung-2 tab INLP deletes the directions a probe uses and every bio is rerun. At high ρ the cue tracks gender so closely that a fresh probe still finds gender through it, so INLP deletes the cue direction too, and predictions move in both worlds. “Zeroing it changes the prediction on these bios” then holds in both; “relies on gender” holds only in the world whose readout uses gender.

The rung-3 tab takes one bio. Rerunning it with a projected gives the same number as its row on the rung-2 tab, and with the input given that number is the bio's counterfactual about a: moving the claim about a from rung 2 to rung 3 costs nothing. The question in the tab's title is about gender, and here rung 3 adds the concept. The rerun answers it only if the deleted directions are gender alone and setting them to zero puts a at the he value. This toy meets the first condition at ρ ≤ 0.5 and the second only because it codes he as 0; a real model tells you neither, and projection there sets the direction to zero, which for centred activations is a value no bio had. The twin bio is the other route: rewrite she as he and rerun, which answers the question only if the rewrite changes gender and nothing else (δ = 0).

Try this

  1. On the rung-1 tab, switch between World A and World B before revealing anything. Which number changes? Write down the one sentence the probe's accuracy supports, then press “Reveal the readout” and check whether it survived in both worlds.
  2. On the rung-2 tab at ρ = 0.9, note the share of flipped predictions in each world. Set ρ = 0.5 and look again. In which world did the number collapse, and what does the count of deleted directions tell you about why? Would a stricter stopping rule than 75% have changed the answer?
  3. On the rung-3 tab, press Reveal, then try ρ = 0.5 with δ = 0. Which of ① and ③ now matches ②? On real Bias-in-Bios data, which of these conditions could you check, and with what experiment?
